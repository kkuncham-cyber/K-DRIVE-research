# Supplementary methods and data dictionary — K-DRIVE (24 October 2026)

## Data provenance and the two distinct study arms

Observed: `repository/data/survey_public_projection.csv` has 306 anonymized response rows extracted, in original order, from the public safe dataset at https://github.com/antonioclim/SEMANTICorr-AI-Readiness-SME (main branch, `data/public/public_anonymised_dataset.csv`). DOI https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21235494 (Clim, 2026, v2.1.2), data CC BY 4.0. This research derivative retains only 12 survey fields plus a non-identifying row identifier. No persons or businesses are identified. Six of the source survey's constructs were not used; 174 of 306 participants are students, and the dataset is not a representative firm-level survey. 

The original public categorical codings are translated to integer categories. `data_access_challenges_code`: 0 never, 1 rarely, 2 occasionally, 3 frequently, 4 not applicable; `workforce_preparation_code`: 0 no steps yet, 1 planning to, 2 some initial steps, 3 comprehensive training, 4 not applicable; `sector_technology_code`: 0 non-technology/other, 1 technology/IT; `student_code`: 0 nonstudent, 1 student; `investment_intention_code`: 0 no plans, 1 unlikely, 2 uncertain, 3 considering it, 4 yes definitely. Seven numeric attitude/perception variables are recorded on original 1–10 scales, with 15 as a transport sentinel for missing data (replaced with NaN inside empirical.py).

The observed target is `investment_intention_code>=3`; it is not future investment, enterprise readiness, profitability or a treatment outcome. It was chosen as a stated-intention proxy. The selection process used five-fold Brier on 229 training records; held-out 77 respondents were not used for model selection.

Synthetic: `repository/main.py` generates 6,000 artificial firms (3,600 train, 1,200 validation, 1,200 test) and 1,500 independent shifted artificial firms. Artificial outputs are NOT matched to surveyed respondents. Costs are made-up scenario utility units.

## Validation and uncertainty

Real study: Survey AUC and average precision were bootstrapped with 2,000 held-out resamples. The small holdout produces broad intervals; no population-inference or prospective outcome claim is supported. Categorical missing codes are treated as categories rather than numeric values. Preprocessing, when required, is fitted inside CV folds, avoiding label leakage. Selection uses five-fold training-only Brier; all four model test metrics are disclosed even though selection was performed before test inspection. `student_status` can capture convenience-sample composition rather than valid firm capability.

Scenario study: Four action policies use the exact same artificial test records and action-loss function, and the paired bootstrap was 2,000 draws. Model selection uses validation Brier before test evaluation. Decision loss, rule compliance and rates are derived from the same simulated payoffs, so effectiveness should not be inferred for actual enterprises.

## Explicit action matrix

| Action | Simulated ready loss | Simulated not-ready loss | Feasibility |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Launch | 2 | 22 | data>=0.42; cyber>=0.45; budget>=0.38 |
| Pilot | 6 | 9 | budget>=0.28 |
| Defer | 12 | 1 | always |

Unsafe launch penalty in reference objective = 8 units. A penalty sensitivity sweep 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12 and 20 and common launch-threshold offsets −0.05, 0, +0.05 was executed. At penalty 0 the gated policy has higher simulated loss than the rule-free policy, demonstrating sensitivity to assumptions. The sweep is a result, not an after-the-fact claim of robustness.
